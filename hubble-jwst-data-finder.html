    What this tool does

    The Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope have photographed thousands of galaxies, nebulae and star clusters. Almost all of that data is public — but the official archive can be overwhelming. This tool searches the same official data and presents it in plain English.

    Where the data comes from

    Every search and download goes to the MAST archive (Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes) at the Space Telescope Science Institute — the official home of Hubble and JWST data. Nothing is re-hosted here.

    What FITS files are

    Hubble FITS files and JWST FITS files are the standard professional astronomy format. They contain the image itself plus metadata: telescope, instrument, filter, exposure time and sky position. You open them in processing software, not a normal image viewer.

    Calibrated vs raw data

    Raw exposures need a full calibration pipeline. Calibrated exposures have had instrument effects removed. Combined images (JWST i2d, Hubble drc/drz) are calibrated exposures stacked together — usually the best starting point for beginners.

    Which files beginners want

    For image processing, pick the recommended combined images for each filter. One filter per file is normal: you combine them yourself and decide the colours. Skip files labelled raw, preview or metadata unless you know you need them.

    Why filters matter

    A colour image needs several filters of the same target. Broadband filters (like F090W, F200W, F444W) capture wide slices of light; narrowband filters (like F656N or F187N) isolate glowing gas. The tool suggests possible colour mappings, but there is no single correct answer — experimenting is part of the fun.

    Why field overlap matters

    Two observations can use perfect filters but point at slightly different parts of a target. This tool groups nearby pointings and warns when datasets may not cover exactly the same patch of sky. The check uses approximate field centres, not exact footprint polygons, so always sanity-check before long processing sessions.

    Processing the data

    Downloaded FITS files open directly in Siril (free) and PixInsight, and in Photoshop with plugins. A typical JWST workflow: open one i2d file per filter, stretch each to reveal detail, assign filters to red/green/blue, then combine and adjust colour balance.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Hubble and JWST data really free to download?

    Yes. Once an observation's exclusive access period ends, the data becomes public on the MAST archive. Anyone can download the FITS files for free and process them into images, including for astrophotography projects and publications with credit to STScI.

    Which files should a beginner download?

    Start with the combined, calibrated images: look for i2d files for JWST and drc or drz files for Hubble. They are already calibrated and combined, so you can open them directly in Siril or PixInsight. This tool marks those as recommended.

    What is a FITS file?

    FITS (Flexible Image Transport System) is the standard file format for professional astronomy. It stores the image pixels plus metadata such as telescope, instrument, filter, exposure time and coordinates. Siril, PixInsight, GIMP (with plugins) and FITS Liberator can all open them.

    Why do filters matter when downloading data?

    Each filter records a different slice of light. Colour images are created by assigning different filters to the red, green and blue channels. To make a colour picture of a target you normally need data through at least three different filters, ideally of the same region.

    Where does the data come from?

    All results and downloads come directly from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) at the Space Telescope Science Institute, the official archive for Hubble and JWST. Our Astro Journey never re-hosts or modifies the science files.

    Can I share a search with someone?

    Yes. After searching, the page URL updates with your target, telescope and mode (for example ?target=M51&telescope=jwst). Copy the address bar to share the exact search.
