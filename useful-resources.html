YouTube channels worth following

These creators are beginner-friendly and cover imaging, equipment choices, and processing workflows.

  • AstroBiscuit - Practical, entertaining astrophotography and astronomy content with clear explanations.
  • AstroBackyard - Beginner-to-intermediate imaging tutorials, gear setups, and processing walkthroughs.
  • Nebula Photos - Step-by-step guides with strong focus on real beginner workflows.
  • Cuiv, The Lazy Geek - Deep-dive videos on gear, acquisition, and advanced processing concepts.

Free data for processing practice

These archives provide free astronomy datasets, often in FITS format, so you can practice stacking and processing.

  • MAST (Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes) - Free public data from Hubble, JWST, TESS, and more.
  • ESA Sky - Explore and download survey and mission data from ESA archives.
  • ESO Science Archive - Public raw and processed data from ESO observatories.
  • NOIRLab Astro Data Archive - Open access astronomical observations and survey products.
  • Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) - Free calibrated imaging and spectroscopy datasets.

Free software tools

Solid free tools for calibration, stacking, image cleanup, and editing.

  • Siril - Free astrophotography pre-processing and processing software for calibration, stacking, and enhancement.
  • GIMP - Free image editor for finishing touches, layer work, and final presentation edits.
  • DeepSkyStacker - Free stacking software (Windows) for combining light, dark, flat, and bias frames.
  • GraXpert - Free gradient removal tool to reduce light pollution and uneven background glow.
  • Stellarium - Free planetarium software for planning sessions and framing targets.

Note

Some archive portals are broad scientific repositories, so finding beginner-friendly targets can take a little time at first. This page will keep improving as we test more resources.
