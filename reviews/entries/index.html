DwarfLabs Dwarf Mini Review

What It Is

The Dwarf mini is a small smart telescope. It can be used to photograph and video in multiple formats while controlling everything from your phone or tablet.

This allows you to capture different things from birds in a tree to another galaxy.

The Dwarf mini also processes the final image for solar system and deep sky objects for you and you can then download the dinal image to you device ready to share.

The app itself has lots of great features where you get to be a part of the comunity.

Specs and they mean for you

Dimenshions
140mm Height, 115mm width and 45mm depth
The Dwarf Mini is about the size of a thick paperback book, making it easy to carry in a backpack or take on a holiday.
Weight
1.3kg
About the same weight as a 1.25l bottle of water. Light enough to carry comfortably but heavy enough to feel sturdy.
Aperture
50mm
This is the size of the front lense. Think of it as the telescopes light bucket. A larger aperture collects more light, helping you capture fainter targets.
Focal Length
100mm
This determines how "zoomed in" the view is. Short focal lengths provide a wider view but targets can look smaller, while longer focal lengths gives you a more zoomed in view. This can make it harder to find and track.
Battery
Built in rechareable battery via USB-C (up to 4 hours)
Long enough for most observing sessions but always have a fully charged power bank handy.
Storage
64GB Internal storage
Plenty of space for multiple sessions but the Dwarf Mini has to option for memory upgrades such as a memory card, so you will need to move some data via the included usb-c cable to a computer.
Filters
Solar Filter, Dual Band Filter, Astro Filter
The included Solar Filter is required to safely observe the sun. The Duel Band Filter is best for nebulae. The Astro Filter is the general purpose filter for everything else.

The Setup

The setup was quick and simple. You can either scan the QR code from the leaflet that comes in the box or straight from the app store. You then either sign in or sign up.

You then just need to follow the on-screen instructions to connect to your telescope.

Depending on when and what you are imaging depends on what you do in the app.

If you are imaging the sun, the first thing you need to do is extend the lense arm and attatch the solar filter before aiming at the sun.

Then in the app, you select the live view, up in the top right you select the small planets icon and from there is where you can select what type of target your looking to capture,

From there you can select the solar system for anything inside the solar system and so on.

Sessions

NGC7000

This is a fairly brief attempt at NGC7000> Also known as C20 and more comonly as North American Nebula.

I used the standard settings over a few different nights. As a typical UK night, there was lots of cloud.

One of the great features of the Dwarf Mini is mega stack.

What this does is if you cant get lots of data in one session, you con combine all the other session on the same target into one.

Now this is very important as the more data you have, the better the finished image would look. It helps to clear out background noise such as light pollution.

I only managed less than 2 hours of data in total and that does show in the image.

With more time the dust clouds would have been fuch more visable. And as there are also 3 street lights close to where i was imaging, more data would have helped quite a bit.

Advanced Features

I'm only going to cover these features here in brief as I have not had the chance to try these properly.

Mega stack I covered briefly but this is one of my favourite features. It is especially useful for us in the UK as we dont always get the best sky conditions so see this getting used alot.

This feature can be found under album.

The next feature is Stellar Studeo.

This is where you can play about with the image a bit to improve it. You can do this manually or in auto.

This is also in the album.

Co Stack and CoMosaic are two features I am looking forward to trying.

CoStack is where you can create or join comunity projects where upto 25 people can take part. Each user captures the same target with the same settings and eventually all the images are stacked. Each user gets their own dta and the final image.

Co Mosaic is a similar sort of thing but with this, as a comunity you can creat a much larger mosaic so you should be able to cover a much bigger area of sky.

I love the idea of both of these features but do see some future options that would be useful. Such as being able to create projects where other users can only take part if you have sent them some for of request or private login.

Also having the option for each individual to share their data would allow for everyont to be able to process the entire project outside of the dwarf app.

You are also able to change the image settings so you can control the exposure time, gain and image count amongst others so you have more control over the image.

This brings us onto one of the most advanced parts that can make a complete difference to you imaging. This is EQ Mode, (Equitorial Mode) or polar alignment

This feature is not completely necessary but if you want longer exposures, you do need it.

Without EQ mode, exposure can go to 30 seconds but due to Field RotationField rotation is the apparent turning of the sky during a long exposure. An equatorial mount tracks with Earth's rotation, helping keep stars round., it's recommended to stick around 10-15 seconds.

With EQ Mode, in the perfect conditions this can be extended to 3 minutes but up to 90 seconds is recommended.
