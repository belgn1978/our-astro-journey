How to use it

  1. Enter a target. Search for an object such as M51, the Orion Nebula, M42, NGC 7000 or the Pillars of Creation.
  2. Choose a telescope. Search Hubble, JWST or both.
  3. Choose what you need. For most people, “Recommended data for image processing” is the best starting point.
  4. Review the recommended datasets. The tool groups observations, filters and instruments, and explains which combinations may be useful for colour processing.
  5. Add files to your download basket. Select individual files or use Add recommended files to let the tool choose the best starting files for that dataset.
  6. Open the download basket. Each file in the basket has its own direct MAST download link. Tapping or clicking Download starts the download for that one FITS file. If your basket contains three files, you have three separate direct file downloads.
  7. Use the bulk options only if you want them. Save all download links (.txt) creates a text file containing one direct FITS-file link per line. Copy all download links copies those same direct links to your clipboard. Download all with a script (.sh) creates a small script that follows every link and downloads the whole basket automatically.
  8. Process the FITS files on a computer. Keep the files for the same target together, then open them in software such as Siril or PixInsight. Phones are fine for searching and building the basket, but the actual FITS files can be very large.

The files do not come from Our Astro Journey and are not hosted by us. Every download comes from the official MAST archive — this tool simply helps you locate and organise the correct public datasets.

Large download warning

Hubble and JWST FITS datasets can range from hundreds of megabytes to many gigabytes. We recommend using this tool on a desktop or laptop with plenty of free storage rather than downloading large datasets directly to a phone or tablet.

  • Check the estimated basket size before downloading.
  • FITS files are intended for astronomy and image-processing software, not normal photo viewers.
  • For bigger datasets, an external SSD or other large storage device is a good idea.

    What is the Hubble & JWST Image Data Downloader?

    The Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope have photographed thousands of galaxies, nebulae and star clusters. Almost all of that image data is public — but the official archive can be overwhelming. This free tool searches the same official data, groups it into sensible datasets, and explains in plain English which Hubble FITS files or JWST FITS files are worth downloading for astrophotography processing.

    Where does the data come from?

    Every search and download goes to the MAST archive (Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes) at the Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI) — the official home of Hubble and JWST data. Nothing is re-hosted, modified or proxied by Our Astro Journey.

    What are FITS files?

    Hubble FITS files and JWST FITS files use the standard professional astronomy format. Each file contains the image itself plus metadata: telescope, instrument, filter, exposure time and sky position. You open them in processing software such as Siril or PixInsight, not a normal image viewer.

    Which files should beginners download?

    For JWST image processing, start with the combined i2d images. For Hubble image processing, look for drizzle-combined drc or drz files. These are already calibrated and stacked, so they open directly in Siril or PixInsight. The tool marks these as recommended and explains why.

    Raw, calibrated or combined — what's the difference?

    Raw data needs a full calibration pipeline before it looks like an image. Calibrated exposures have had instrument effects removed but are still separate frames. Combined images are calibrated exposures stacked together — the usual best starting point when you download Hubble data or download JWST data for a colour picture.

    How do filters create colour images?

    A colour image needs several filters of the same target. Broadband filters (like F090W, F200W, F444W) capture wide slices of light; narrowband filters (like F656N or F187N) isolate glowing gas. You assign one filter to each colour channel — the tool suggests possible mappings, but there is no single correct answer, and experimenting is part of the fun.

    Why does field overlap matter?

    Two observations can use perfect filters but point at slightly different parts of a target. This tool groups nearby pointings and warns when datasets may not cover exactly the same patch of sky. The check uses approximate field centres, not exact footprint polygons, so always sanity-check before long processing sessions.

    How large are the downloads?

    A single combined JWST image is often over 100 MB, and a full multi-filter dataset can be several gigabytes. The download basket shows the estimated total size before you start, and warns you when a dataset is large enough to prefer a desktop connection or an external SSD.

    What software can open FITS files?

    Downloaded data opens directly in Siril (free) and PixInsight, and in Photoshop with plugins. A typical JWST workflow: open one combined image per filter, stretch each to reveal detail, assign filters to red, green and blue, then combine and adjust the colour balance. The same approach works for Hubble data for astrophotography.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I download JWST images for free?

    Yes. Once an observation's exclusive access period ends, JWST data becomes public on the MAST archive. Anyone can download the FITS files for free, including for astrophotography processing projects and publications with credit to STScI.

    Can I download Hubble images for free?

    Yes. Public Hubble data is free to download from the MAST archive. This tool helps you find the right observations and files; the downloads come directly from the official archive.

    Where does this tool get its data?

    All results and downloads come directly from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) at the Space Telescope Science Institute, the official archive for Hubble and JWST. Our Astro Journey never re-hosts or modifies the science files.

    What are FITS files?

    FITS (Flexible Image Transport System) is the standard file format for professional astronomy. It stores the image pixels plus metadata such as telescope, instrument, filter, exposure time and coordinates. Siril, PixInsight, FITS Liberator and Photoshop (with plugins) can all open them.

    Which JWST files should I download for image processing?

    Start with the combined i2d images — one per filter. They are already calibrated and stacked, so you can open them directly in Siril or PixInsight and combine filters into a colour image. The tool marks these as recommended.

    Which Hubble files should I download?

    Look for drizzle-combined drc or drz files — one per filter. These are the calibrated, combined Hubble images most beginners want. Individual calibrated (flc/flt) exposures are available in advanced modes if you want to combine them yourself.

    Do I need a powerful computer?

    Searching works on any device. Processing large FITS files is more comfortable on a desktop or laptop with enough RAM and free disk space, especially for multi-gigabyte JWST datasets.

    How much storage space do JWST files need?

    Individual combined JWST images are often 100 MB or more each, and full multi-filter datasets can reach several gigabytes. Check the estimated size in the download basket before starting, and consider an external SSD for bigger projects.

    Can I use this tool on a phone?

    Yes — searching and building a download list is fully mobile friendly. For the actual downloads, a desktop or laptop is recommended because the files can be very large.

    Can I process JWST and Hubble data in Siril?

    Yes. Siril is free and opens FITS files directly. A typical workflow is to open one combined image per filter, stretch each one, then assign filters to the red, green and blue channels to build a colour image.

    Can I use PixInsight?

    Yes. PixInsight opens Hubble and JWST FITS files directly and is a popular choice for combining filters and colour mapping. The free FITS Liberator plugin also lets Photoshop work with the same files.

    Why do some filters not line up perfectly?

    Observations through different filters can be taken at different times, from slightly different pointings, or with different instruments. This tool warns you when datasets may not cover exactly the same field so you can check footprints before combining them.

    What is MAST?

    MAST — the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes — is the official NASA archive for Hubble, JWST and other missions, run by the Space Telescope Science Institute. It stores the raw and calibrated science data and serves the actual downloads.

    Can I share a search with someone?

    Yes. After searching, the page URL updates with your target, telescope and mode (for example ?target=M51&telescope=jwst). Copy the address bar to share the exact search.
